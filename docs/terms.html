5. Calculator estimates and accuracy of content

The calculator

The buying power calculator gives rough estimates to help you plan. It is not a mortgage offer, an approval in principle or a quote.

The calculator uses simplified rules. For example, it uses:

  • a loan limit of 4 times gross income for first-time buyers and 3.5 times for other buyers;
  • a minimum deposit of 10% of the price;
  • stamp duty at Revenue's residential rates (1% up to €1 million, 2% up to €1.5 million and 6% above), worked out on the price without VAT for a new home;
  • a simplified Help to Buy check: first-time buyers of a new home up to €500,000, with a mortgage of at least 70% of the price; and
  • typical figures for costs such as solicitor, survey and valuation fees.

Your real figures will depend on your circumstances, the lender, the property and the rules in force at the time. A lender will make its own assessment and may lend you more or less than the calculator shows. Monthly repayment figures depend on the interest rate and term you enter.

The figures you enter in the calculator, such as your salary, savings and target price, stay in your browser. They are not sent to us.

The guide and other content

We try to keep the content accurate and up to date. However, rules, rates, schemes and procedures change, sometimes at short notice, and the site may not reflect the latest position. The content is also simplified and may not cover every situation.

Before you rely on anything, check it with official sources, such as:

  • the Central Bank of Ireland, for mortgage lending rules and to check whether a firm is regulated;
  • Revenue, for stamp duty, Help to Buy and other tax matters; and
  • Citizens Information, for general information about buying a home and your rights.

If you spot something that is wrong or out of date, please tell us at eirehomeflow@gmail.com.
