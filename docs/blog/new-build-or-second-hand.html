New build or second-hand: how the numbers change

The same budget goes further on some homes than on others, and new builds and second-hand homes differ in more than age. Here is how the numbers and the timeline change.

2 min read

Side by side

TopicNew buildSecond-hand
Help to BuyUp to €30,000 or 10% of the priceNot available
First Home SchemeAvailable below the local price ceilingNot available
Stamp duty1% of the price without VAT1% of the price
Energy ratingUsually A-ratedAnything from A to G
Green mortgage rateUsually availableOnly with a BER of B3 or better
TimelineCan take months, until the home is finishedOften 3-4 months from Sale Agreed
Checks before closingA snag listA structural survey

The case for a new build

  • Help to Buy and the First Home Scheme can close a deposit gap that would take years to save.
  • Lower energy bills and access to a green mortgage rate.
  • A structural guarantee for 10 years, usually from HomeBond or Premier Guarantee.
  • Less maintenance in the first years.

The case for a second-hand home

  • More choice of location, including established areas close to work and schools.
  • Larger gardens and rooms for the price in many areas.
  • A shorter wait: you can often move in within months.
  • Room to add value by upgrading, with SEAI grants for insulation and heating.

The trade-off is condition. Budget for a survey, and for the work that the BER and the survey point to.

Questions for a developer

  • When is completion expected, and what happens if it is late?
  • Is the developer registered for Help to Buy?
  • Which structural guarantee comes with the home?
  • What is included: flooring, wardrobes, appliances, landscaping?
  • For an apartment or a managed estate, what will the management fee be?

Try both options in the calculator, which shows how Help to Buy and stamp duty change the numbers, and save the one you choose to My journey.

Worth knowing

The booking deposit on a new home is refundable until contracts are signed. Get the terms in writing before you pay.
