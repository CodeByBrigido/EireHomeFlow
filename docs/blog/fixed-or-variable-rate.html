Fixed, variable or green: choosing your mortgage rate

The type of rate shapes your repayments for years, as much as the rate itself. Irish lenders offer fixed periods from one year to ten or more, variable rates, and lower green rates for energy-efficient homes.

2 min read

Fixed rates

A fixed rate keeps your repayment the same for the fixed period, whatever happens to market rates. It suits a tight budget, and buyers who want certainty in the first years, when the loan is at its largest.

  • Leaving a fixed rate early, to switch lender or clear a large part of the loan, can cost a break fee.
  • Some lenders allow limited overpayments during a fixed period without a fee. Ask for the exact limit.
  • When the period ends you move to a new fixed or variable rate, so note the date.

Variable rates

A variable rate can rise or fall when the lender decides, usually following the European Central Bank. It is more flexible: you can overpay or switch lender without a break fee.

It suits buyers who expect to overpay, sell or switch within a few years, and who could manage a higher repayment if rates went up.

Green mortgages

Most Irish lenders offer a lower rate for homes with a BER of B3 or better. The discount is often a few tenths of a percentage point, which over a fixed period can add up to thousands of euro.

New builds usually qualify. For a second-hand home, the BER on the listing tells you whether a green rate is possible, or how far an upgrade would need to go.

Comparing offers

  • Compare the APRC, which includes fees, as well as the headline rate.
  • Check what happens when a fixed period ends. Some lenders move existing customers to rates above what they offer new ones.
  • Cashback can help with furnishing, but compare the total cost over the fixed period.
  • A mortgage broker can compare several lenders for you. Ask how they are paid.

Worth knowing

On a €300,000 mortgage over 30 years, 3.65% instead of 3.9% saves about €43 a month, or more than €15,000 over the whole term.
